    Cameras

    AUTOMATA has two kinds of view:

    • The editor view is the free orbit camera you navigate with in Oberon. It isn't part of the scene and is never saved.
    • Scene cameras are entities with a Camera component. They're saved in the scene file. Play mode and the shipped game render through the first one.

    Add a camera#

    + → Camera in the Hierarchy, or GameObject → Camera. You can also use Graphics → Camera → Add Camera. You can add a Camera component to any entity too: Add Component → Camera.

    A new camera is placed where you're currently looking from, aimed at the same point, so it starts with a sensible view.

    Camera fields#

    FieldMeaning
    Field of ViewVertical angle in degrees. Around 60 is natural; 30 is a telephoto zoom; 90+ is a wide action view
    Near Clip / Far ClipNothing closer than Near or farther than Far is drawn. Keep Near as large as you can (0.1–0.5) to avoid depth flicker
    EyeWhere the camera is
    Look AtThe point it aims at

    The camera aims at a point rather than storing a rotation, because that matches how games usually move cameras: follow something, look at something.

    Moving the entity moves the camera. When you drag a camera with the Move gizmo, or change its Transform position, Eye and Look At move with it. That keeps the aim. Editing Eye or Look At directly doesn't drag the Transform along.

    See what the camera sees#

    • Frustum. Every camera draws its view pyramid in the scene view. The selected one is brighter, and a small notch marks the top edge.
    • Preview. While a camera is selected, a live render of its view appears in the bottom-right corner of the scene view. You can turn it off with Graphics → Camera → Preview. It hides automatically when the view is too small.
    • Look Through jumps the editor view to the camera's position, aim, and field of view. It's the quickest way to frame a shot exactly.
    • Align to View does the reverse: it moves the camera to where the editor view is now. A good workflow is to navigate until the shot looks right, then click Align to View. It's one undo step.

    Look Through and Align to View are in the Inspector's Camera section and in Graphics → Camera.

    Cameras in Play mode#

    When the scene has a camera, Play renders through the first one in the hierarchy. The editor's own view is ignored until you Stop. The built-in player's WASD movement is relative to the camera's facing.

    With no camera, Play uses the editor view and follows the player. That's handy for quick tests, but a shipped game should always have a camera.

    Driving a camera from a script#

    Attach a script to the camera entity, or to anything else, and use entity.camera or ctx.camera:

    def update(entity, ctx):
    cam = ctx.camera                     # the camera Play renders through
    cam.follow(ctx.scene["Player"])      # smooth third-person follow
    MemberMeaning
    cam.positionEye (x, y, z). Read it or assign it
    cam.targetLook-at point (x, y, z)
    cam.fovVertical field of view in degrees (clamped to 1–179)
    cam.look_at(point_or_entity)Aim at a point or at an entity's position
    cam.follow(entity, offset=(0, 3, 8), smooth=8.0, look_height=0.6)Ease toward entity + offset and look at the entity. Call it every frame. smooth=0 snaps

    The same API works in Oberon's Play mode and under at.run_scene.

    Recipes#

    Fixed security camera that tracks the player

    def update(entity, ctx):
    entity.camera.look_at(ctx.scene["Player"])

    Side-scroller: lock the camera's Z distance and follow only X and Y.

    def update(entity, ctx):
    x, y, _ = ctx.scene["Player"].transform.position
    cam = entity.camera
    cx, cy, cz = cam.position
    k = min(1.0, 6.0 * ctx.time.delta)
    cam.position = (cx + (x - cx) * k, cy + (y + 1.5 - cy) * k, 12.0)
    cam.target = (cam.position[0], cam.position[1] - 1.0, 0.0)

    Orbit with Q / E

    import math

def start(entity, ctx):
    entity.state["yaw"] = 0.0

def update(entity, ctx):
    yaw = entity.state["yaw"]
    yaw += (ctx.input.pressed("e") - ctx.input.pressed("q")) * 2.0 * ctx.time.delta
    entity.state["yaw"] = yaw
    px, py, pz = ctx.scene["Player"].transform.position
    entity.camera.position = (px + 8 * math.sin(yaw), py + 3.5, pz + 8 * math.cos(yaw))
    entity.camera.look_at((px, py + 0.6, pz))

    Zoom while a key is held (Z)

    def update(entity, ctx):
    goal = 35.0 if ctx.input.pressed("z") else 60.0
    entity.camera.fov += (goal - entity.camera.fov) * min(1.0, 10 * ctx.time.delta)
