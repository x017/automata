    Guide: Physics

    Rapier-powered rigid bodies in 3D and 2D, a character controller, and raycasts — stepped at a fixed 60 Hz so gameplay is identical at any frame rate.

    Bodies#

    at.RigidBody.dynamic()              # gravity, forces, collisions — crates, balls
at.RigidBody.fixed()                # never moves — floors, walls, platforms
at.RigidBody.kinematic_velocity()   # you set velocity; physics resolves collisions
at.RigidBody.kinematic_position()   # you set position; it pushes dynamic bodies

    Fine-tuning: `at.RigidBody(body_type="dynamic", gravity_scale=1.0, linear_damping=0.0, angular_damping=0.0, enabled=True)`.

    Colliders#

    at.Collider3D.cuboid(x, y, z)   # FULL sizes — match Transform.scale exactly
at.Collider3D.ball(radius)
at.Collider3D.capsule(half_height, radius)
at.Collider3D.mesh("models/platform.glb")         # the model's own triangles
at.Collider3D.convex_hull("models/crate.glb")     # shrink-wrapped around it

at.Collider2D.cuboid(x, y)
at.Collider2D.ball(radius)
at.Collider2D.capsule(half_height, radius)

    Chainable material modifiers: .friction(0.8), .restitution(0.3) (bounciness), .density(2.0), .sensor(True) (detect overlap without blocking movement — pickups, triggers).

    Collider sizes are full sizes. Transform(scale=(40, 1, 40)) pairs with Collider3D.cuboid(40, 1, 40). Get this wrong and things float or sink by exactly half.

    Colliders from models#

    mesh and convex_hull take a model file (relative to the scene, like MeshRenderer.model) and build the shape from it when the scene starts, after the model's import settings and at the entity's scale. The scene stores only the path.

    • Mesh is exact: rays and characters hit the real triangles, so ramps, arches and bevelled platforms behave as they look. Use it for static scenery (no rigid body, or a static or kinematic one). A triangle mesh has no inside, so it can't give a dynamic body mass: on a dynamic body it plays as its convex hull, and Oberon and Build Game flag it.
    • Convex hull wraps the model like shrink-film: no dents or holes. Use it for dynamic bodies a box or capsule fits badly.
    • Boxes, balls and capsules are still the cheapest. Prefer them when they fit.

    2D and 3D are separate worlds. A Collider2D never touches a Collider3D. Pick one per game; per entity, use the matching family.

    The per-frame toolbox#

    ctx.physics.grounded(player)                       # standing on something?
ctx.physics.velocity(entity)                       # current (vx, vy, vz)
ctx.physics.set_velocity(entity, (0, 5, 0))
ctx.physics.apply_impulse(entity, (0, 300, 0))     # one-shot kick
ctx.physics.apply_force(entity, (10, 0, 0))        # continuous push
ctx.physics.gravity() / ctx.physics.set_gravity((0, -9.81, 0))
ctx.physics.body_count()

key = ctx.physics.cast_ray(origin, direction, max_distance=100.0, exclude=player)
hit = ctx.physics.ray_result(key)   # (entity_id, point, normal, distance) or None
ctx.physics.water_height(x, z)      # water surface height there, or None
ctx.water.splash(entity, 2.0)       # rings and foam (scene scripts; see Water)

    ctx.physics2d mirrors the essentials for the 2D world (apply_impulse, grounded, body_count).

    Water#

    Every entity with a Water component is also a volume of water. Dynamic bodies in it get two things each step:

    • Buoyancy: an upward push of `water density × gravity × submerged volume`, applied where the body is under, so a body settles at the depth its density gives and tilts with the waves. A body floats when its collider density is below the water's, with that fraction under.
    • Drag: it loses speed and spin in proportion to how much of it is under (drag, default 1.0; 0 turns it off).
    scene.add(at.Entity("sea").add(at.Transform()).add(
    at.Water(size=(80, 80), wave_height=0.4, wave_length=20.0)))  # density=2.0
scene.add(at.Entity("crate")
    .add(at.Transform(position=(0, 2, 0)))
    .add(at.MeshRenderer.cube(size=1.0))
    .add(at.RigidBody.dynamic())
    .add(at.Collider3D.cuboid(1, 1, 1)))              # density 1.0: half under
scene.add(at.Entity("anchor")
    .add(at.Transform(position=(3, 2, 0)))
    .add(at.MeshRenderer.cube(size=0.5))
    .add(at.RigidBody.dynamic())
    .add(at.Collider3D.cuboid(0.5, 0.5, 0.5).density(6.0)))  # sinks, slowly

    Bodies ride exactly the waves that are drawn: physics samples the same Gerstner waves on the CPU, on the same clock. A long wave over a large surface moves the geometry. Waves shorter than about four of the surface's 128 grid cells only ripple the shading, so they don't move bodies either. In Oberon, set Density and Drag in the Water section of the Inspector.

    It's built to be cheap:

    • Without water it costs nothing.
    • Sleeping bodies are skipped, and bodies that settle on calm water fall asleep.
    • Awake bodies away from the water cost a couple of comparisons.
    • A body in the water is sampled at 1 point if small, or 8 if not (so long hulls pitch and roll). About 0.25 µs per floating body per step on waves, measured on one core.

    The surface isn't solid: give the water a floor if sinking things should stop. The Lagoon example floats crates and buoys, and runs a boat round the island.

    Ripples and wakes#

    On top of the waves, a small wave simulation runs around the camera: a 256 × 256 grid, a quarter metre per cell (64 m across), that follows the view. Anything crossing the surface pushes it about, dynamic and kinematic bodies alike:

    • A body that drops in splashes: rings spread out from it, and foam. The harder and bigger, the bigger the splash.
    • A body that bobs sends out small rings as it bobs.
    • A body that moves pushes ripples ahead and leaves a V-shaped wake behind it, with a trail of foam. Ripples spread at 1.6 m/s, so anything faster than that (a boat) gets a proper V.
    • Scripts make their own: ctx.water.splash(position, strength) (scene scripts, Oberon included) or ctx.physics.splash((x, y, z), strength) (engine scripts). position can be an entity; strength runs from 0 to 10, and 1 is about a crate dropped from a couple of metres.
    def update(entity, ctx):
    if ctx.input.just_pressed("space"):
        ctx.water.splash(entity, 3.0)   # a cannonball lands here

    Ripples are only drawn; they don't move bodies (those ride the waves). They fade over about ten seconds. Beyond the 64 m around the view there are none, and the field fades out toward its edges so it never ends in a seam.

    Splashes and spray#

    A body that hits the water faster than 1.5 m/s also throws up a splash: a burst of droplets (the "splash" particle preset) where it went in. Its size follows the entry speed. A crate dropped from a metre makes a few droplets; a dive off a pier sends up a white crown. Characters splash too, although they're moved by teleporting rather than by velocity.

    On a Water with wind above 0.5, spray blows off the wave crests within 25 m of the camera, carried downwind (wave_direction). It gets more frequent and denser as wind goes up to 1. Calm water never sprays.

    Both are ordinary particle entities that remove themselves once they're done. In Rust, PhysicsTick::splashes lists each step's splashes (where, how fast and how big), and set_water_particles(false) stops the particles but keeps the rings.

    It costs nothing until something disturbs the water and nothing again once it calms down. While it runs, about 0.25 ms per physics step in a release build. The engine and Oberon point it at the camera that draws the frame; with your own PhysicsWorld3D in Rust, call `set_ripple_view(eye, target)each frame and handripples()toDrawList::set_ripples`.

    The character controller#

    The platformer recipe — tight movement that still collides and climbs slopes:

    player = (
    at.Entity("player")
    .add(at.Transform(position=(0, 1.4, 0)))
    .add(at.MeshRenderer.cube(size=0.9, color=ORANGE))
    .add(at.RigidBody.kinematic_velocity())       # 1. we own the velocity
    .add(at.Collider3D.capsule(0.5, 0.4))         # 2. capsule slides over edges
    .add(at.CharacterController(snap_to_ground=0.5))  # 3. kinematic solver
    .add(at.CharacterVelocity())                  # 4. per-frame drive input
)
    def on_update(self, ctx):
    player = ctx.scene["player"]
    dx = self.controls.axis("move_x", ctx.input)
    if ctx.physics.grounded(player):
        self.vy = -1.0                             # stay snapped to ground
        if self.controls.just_pressed("jump", ctx.input):
            self.vy = JUMP_SPEED
    else:
        self.vy -= GRAVITY * ctx.time.delta
    player.character_velocity = (dx * WALK_SPEED, self.vy, 0.0)

    CharacterController options (keyword-only): snap_to_ground, autostep, max_slope_climb_angle, min_slope_slide_angle, offset, slide, and mode="normal" (or "autostep"-friendly setups).

    Common pitfalls#

    • Player falls through the floor: collider sizes don't match the visual scale (use full sizes), or the floor lacks RigidBody.fixed().
    • Kinematic character never moves: you set Transform.position instead of character_velocity — the solver only sees the velocity component.
    • Dynamic body jitters against a wall: that's restitution/friction; try friction(0.2) on the wall or clamp velocity yourself.
