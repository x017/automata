    Tutorial: Your first scene

    Goal: build a small level in Oberon, light it, place a camera, play it, and save it. No code yet.

    You need: Oberon running (cargo run -p oberon --release). See Oberon.

    1. Start clean#

    Oberon opens with a starter scene: a sun, a ground, a cube, a player, and three crates. Choose File → New Scene (Ctrl+N) to get an empty one instead. The Hierarchy on the left is now empty.

    2. A floor#

    1. Click + at the top of the Hierarchy and choose Cube.
    2. In the Inspector, set Transform → Scale to 20, 0.2, 20 and Position Y to -0.1. Click a field and type, or drag the X / Y / Z label left and right to scrub.
    3. Click the name field at the top of the Inspector and rename the cube Ground. F2 also works.
    4. Make it solid: choose Add Component → Rigidbody and set Body Type to Static. Then choose Add Component → Collider; the collider sizes itself to the mesh.

    (+ → Plane makes a flat visual-only quad. That's good for decoration, but use a cube when things need to stand on it.)

    3. Some objects#

    1. + → Cube. Press W for the Move gizmo and drag the red arrow to move the cube along X.
    2. Press Ctrl+D a few times to duplicate it, and spread the copies around.
    3. Select one cube and click the Color swatch under Mesh Renderer. Pick a colour, or type a hex value such as #E8613A.
    4. Press R for Scale and stretch one cube into a tall pillar.

    Made a mistake? Ctrl+Z undoes it. Every edit is one step, including a whole gizmo drag.

    4. Light#

    Choose + → Directional Light. A sun icon appears in the view, and the objects get light and shadows.

    Open the Graphics tab (right dock, beside Inspector). Under Sun:

    • drag Elevation down to about 25 for long evening shadows
    • drag Azimuth around until the shadows fall where you like them

    5. A player#

    Choose + → Player. You get an orange capsule-sized box with a Character Controller. In Play mode it walks with WASD, jumps with Space, and sprints with Shift. No script is needed for that.

    Move it up a little so it isn't stuck inside the floor. A Y position of 0.9 stands it on the ground.

    6. A camera#

    Orbit the view (drag empty space) and zoom (wheel) until you have a nice shot from behind and above the player. Then choose + → Camera.

    The new camera is placed exactly where you're looking from:

    • a frustum pyramid shows what it sees
    • a preview in the bottom-right corner shows its live view

    Orbit away and the frustum stays put. Click Look Through in the Inspector to jump back into the camera's view. Adjust the shot, then click Align to View to move the camera there.

    7. Play#

    Press ▶ (Ctrl+P).

    • The view switches to your camera.
    • WASD moves the player relative to the camera, and Space jumps.
    • Press ■ to stop. Everything returns to exactly where it was.

    The camera doesn't follow the player yet; you'll script that in Third-person camera. If you delete the camera, Play falls back to the editor view following the player.

    8. Save#

    Ctrl+S asks for a file name. Save as my_game/level.ron (make a new folder for it). The title bar shows the name, and the unsaved-changes dot disappears.

    The file is plain text you can read. It now shows up in Files at the bottom of the left sidebar. Open it there, or in any editor:

    (
    version: 1,
    entities: [
        (
            name: Some("Ground"),
            transform: Some((position: (0.0, 0.0, 0.0), scale: (20.0, 1.0, 20.0), ...)),
            ...

    What you learned#

    • Hierarchy + creates objects; the Inspector edits them.
    • W / E / R gizmos, Ctrl+D duplicate, Ctrl+Z undo.
    • The sun's direction lives in Graphics → Sun.
    • A Camera decides what Play shows, and Look Through / Align to View frame it.
    • Scenes are .ron text files.
